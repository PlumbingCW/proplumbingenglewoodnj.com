Emergency Water Line Repair in Englewood, NJ

Wet yard, low pressure or a surprise water bill? Licensed plumbers find and fix leaks in the buried line between the street and your house in Englewood.

Call (551) 249-3045

The water service line is the buried pipe that brings water from the main in the street to your house, and when it leaks, you may lose pressure or the whole supply. Water line repair in Englewood, NJ means locating the leak underground, then repairing or replacing the pipe. Our line is open 24/7 for breaks that cannot wait. If water is surfacing in your yard, close the main shutoff inside and call us.

Service lines are hard to judge from the surface. A soft patch of lawn, a puddle in winter, a bill that jumped or pressure that sags can all trace to a pipe you cannot see, so we confirm the leak before anyone starts digging.

How you know the buried line is leaking

A leaking service line rarely announces itself loudly. The common signs are a wet or unusually green patch of lawn, a sunken spot over the pipe route, water seeping into the basement where the line enters, or a hiss near the meter.

The water meter is the best tool you own. Turn off every faucet and appliance, then watch the meter's small leak indicator or low-flow dial. If it keeps moving, water is escaping somewhere between the meter and your fixtures, and a leak detection visit can tell the yard from the house.

A sudden jump in your bill with no change in habits is another warning. Ask your water supplier whether they can flag unusual use on your account.

Trenchless or open trench replacement

Both methods end with new pipe in the ground. The difference is how much of your yard is disturbed along the way.

MethodHow it worksTrade-off
Open trenchDig along the whole line, remove old pipe, lay newSimple and visible, but disturbs lawn, walks and driveways
Pipe burstingPull new pipe through the old one, breaking it outwardLittle digging, but needs entry and exit pits and suitable soil
Spot repairDig at the leak and replace a short sectionCheapest when the rest of the pipe is sound, may leave aged pipe in place
ReliningPipe coating or sleeve inside the old lineLess digging, but fit depends on pipe condition, material and what suits drinking water lines
Full replacement at new routeInstall a fresh line along a better pathCosts more but avoids trees, patios and other obstacles

Which method fits depends on pipe material, depth, what sits above the line and what the soil allows.

Water supplier, street connection and local ground

Much of northern Bergen County drinks water from Veolia's Oradell Reservoir system, so check the supplier named on your water bill, and ask that supplier for its annual water quality report. The supplier is also the first call for questions about the main in the street and the meter.

Ownership between main and house varies. In many places the supplier owns the main and curb stop, and the homeowner owns the pipe from there to the house, but arrangements differ, so ask your supplier before you dig or pay for repairs on a pipe you may not own.

Elevation can also matter for pressure. Leonia runs from about 5 feet above sea level in the west to about 318 feet in the east, and low-lying homes in a range like that may see higher static pressure than homes uphill. A pressure reducing valve, if you have one, deserves a check when the service line is opened. See our Leonia plumber page for that area.

Low water pressure is not always the line

Weak pressure in every fixture does point toward the service line, the meter, the main shutoff or a pressure reducing valve. Pressure that is low at only one faucet is usually a clogged aerator or a partly closed stop.

Old galvanized steel service lines narrow as rust builds up inside, which cuts flow even without a leak. A partly open shutoff valve, a failing pressure reducer and a problem on the supplier's side can mimic the same symptoms. We check each before recommending a dig.

Water line broken or water surfacing in the yard

A broken service line can waste large amounts of water and wash out soil around the foundation. Close the main shutoff inside the house first. If water keeps flowing from the yard after that, the break is on the supplier's side of the connection or the shutoff failed, and the supplier should be called as well.

Do not dig on your own. Buried gas, electric and communication lines run through many yards, and a call to 811 before any digging gets them marked. We arrange that marking before we dig.

  • 1Close the main shutoff valve inside the house, usually near the meter
  • 2Note where water appears in the yard and photograph it, including the meter reading
  • 3Keep children and pets away from soft, undermined ground
  • 4Hold off on laundry and dishwashers until the line is checked
  • 5Call the water supplier if water continues after the main is closed
  • 6Ask about drinking water safety if the line has been open or the supply was interrupted

Pipe materials and lead service lines

Service lines have been made of lead, galvanized steel, copper and plastic, depending on when they were installed. Copper and modern plastic lines last many years, while galvanized lines clog and corrode.

Older service lines in any area can include lead. Ask your water supplier whether your line material is on record, and tell us if you plan to replace the line, so we can handle the pipe in a way that suits what is found.

What affects the price of service line work

Length, depth and what sits on top of the pipe decide most of the cost.

Length and depth of the line

A short, shallow run costs far less than a long, deep one.

Repair or full replacement

A spot repair costs less up front, but a worn line may leak again.

Surface restoration

Concrete, pavers and mature landscaping cost more to restore than lawn.

Method

Trenchless work needs specialised tools, while open trench needs more digging and restoration.

Coordination

Marking utilities, supplier connections and inspections add steps and time.

A sudden break in your own service line may be covered under some policies, though many exclude underground lines or require an add-on. Gradual leaks and wear are usually not covered, so ask your carrier whether a service line endorsement exists.
