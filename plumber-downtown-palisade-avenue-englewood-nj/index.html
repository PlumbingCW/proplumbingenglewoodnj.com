Commercial districtDowntown Englewood

Downtown Englewood is a commercial district, not a residential subdivision. The city's Master Plan describes a compact downtown that grew with the railroad, and a 19th-century village that ran mostly along Palisade Avenue from Grand Avenue to Tenafly Road. Today it is a regional destination for retail, dining and culture.

That mix means plumbing calls here often come from businesses and multi-unit buildings rather than single-family houses. This page is for restaurant owners, shop managers, office tenants and landlords. If you live in a house near the downtown, the Liberty Pole page may fit better.

We keep this page separate from our residential pages on purpose. The questions are different, and so are the people who ask them.

Downtown at a glance

Retail, dining and culture

The Master Plan calls downtown a regional destination. Restaurants and shops put heavy, steady demand on floor drains, grease lines and water heaters.

Apartments in the mix

The plan's D-1 subzones allow higher-density housing downtown, and multi-family zones sit on its perimeter. Apartment buildings add shared risers and shared drain lines.

A rail history

Passenger rail reached Englewood in 1859 and the Depot Square station building still stands, though passenger service ended in 1966. Older buildings near the tracks may have been adapted several times.

Offices to the south

South of the downtown along Palisade Avenue, the plan describes an industrial and office district of small and medium businesses.

Working in an occupied building

Plumbing in a downtown building rarely happens in an empty space. A shutoff may affect another tenant, a restaurant may have to close for a repair, and a landlord may need to approve the work. The best calls start with a clear description of who owns which valve.

Tell us whether the building has a single owner or several tenants, which floor the problem is on, and who holds the keys. These details save time and prevent surprises.

Permits and flood context

Commercial work often needs a permit, and the rules vary by job. Ask the Englewood Building & Code Enforcement office at (201) 871-6642, open Monday to Friday, 9:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., excluding holidays before work begins, and tell the plumber what you learn.

The Master Plan rates the downtown and the neighborhoods around Englewood Avenue and West Palisade Avenue as relatively moderate for river flooding, according to the FEMA risk index it cites. That is a citywide planning rating and not a prediction for your building.

Check a plumber before you hire

New Jersey law says plumbing work must be done by a licensed master plumber. Ask for the licence number, then confirm it with the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers, part of the New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs.
