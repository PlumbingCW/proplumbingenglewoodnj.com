Water Heater Installation in Englewood, NJ

Planning a new water heater in Englewood? Licensed plumbers help you size it, choose the fuel and install it correctly.

Water heater installation in Englewood, NJ is the planned job of choosing, placing, connecting and venting a new heater, whether it is a first install in a renovation or a considered upgrade. You have time here to compare options, which is the main advantage over a midnight tank failure.

A good installation depends on choices made before the old tank leaves the house: the right capacity for your household, the right fuel, a safe vent path and a drain for the relief valve. Those choices also decide whether the unit runs quietly for years.

Sizing a heater to the household

Tank size is only half of capacity. The number that matters at 7 a.m. is the first hour rating, which tells you how much hot water the heater can deliver in the first hour of heavy use. Two 50 gallon tanks can have different ratings depending on burner or element power.

Count your peak hour. A family running two showers, a dishwasher and a washing machine at once needs more than a couple that showers separately. Garden tubs and body-spray showers also pull hard. Tank sizes of 40 and 50 gallons are common for family homes, but the plumber should size against your actual habits, not a rule of thumb.

Going too large wastes energy keeping idle water hot. Going too small leaves the last shower cold. A short talk about who lives in the house and when they bathe is worth more than a catalog.

What the installation involves

  1. 01

    Survey the site

    The plumber checks the location, floor, drain, venting path, gas line size or electrical capacity before choosing hardware.

  2. 02

    Remove the old unit

    Water is shut off, the tank drained, the lines and vent disconnected, and the heater taken out.

  3. 03

    Set and plumb

    The new heater sits on a level base or in a drain pan. New flexible connectors, shutoffs and the relief valve pipe go in.

  4. 04

    Connect fuel and vent

    Gas piping is tested for leaks and the flue is sloped and sealed, or power is wired through a correctly sized breaker.

  5. 05

    Fill, start and test

    The tank fills before power or gas goes on. The plumber checks temperature, pressure, flame or element operation and the vent draft.

Gas or electric: picking a fuel

Gas heaters recover quickly and keep working in many outages if they use a standing pilot, though many newer models need electricity for controls. They need a gas line of the right size, a flue, and combustion air. A gas heater in a closet needs adequate air supply.

Electric tank heaters are simpler to install, need no flue and have no combustion, but they recover more slowly and need a dedicated circuit. Switching from gas to electric requires a licensed plumber to cap the old gas line, and an electrician may be needed for the panel.

Heat pump hybrids are electric heaters that use less electricity than a standard electric tank. They need space, airflow and a condensate drain, and they work best in an open basement.

Venting, the flue and the expansion tank

Gas water heaters vent combustion gases outdoors. An atmospheric vent relies on natural draft up a metal flue, while power vent and direct vent models use a fan and plastic or metal pipe through a wall. The plumber chooses based on where the heater sits and where the vent can exit.

Many homes today have a pressure reducing valve or a backflow device on the water service, which turns the plumbing into a closed system. Heating water then raises pressure, and an expansion tank absorbs the extra. It sits on the cold supply line near the heater. Whether yours needs one is a question to put to the plumber.

Every tank heater also needs a temperature and pressure relief valve with a discharge pipe running to a safe point near the floor. Do not cap or plug that pipe.

What affects the price of water heater installation

A planned install lets you control more of these factors than an emergency swap does.

Heater type and capacity

Tank, hybrid and tankless units, and larger capacities, all carry different equipment costs.

Fuel changes

Moving from gas to electric, or the reverse, adds piping or wiring.

Venting work

A new flue, a power vent run, or a wall penetration adds parts and labour.

Added components

Expansion tank, drain pan, new shutoffs and relief valve piping are usually separate line items.

Location

An attic, tight closet or second floor takes longer than an open basement.

Installing a new heater is usually a homeowner expense, not an insurance claim. Resulting water damage from a sudden failure may be covered. Ask your carrier about a planned upgrade and whether a newer unit affects your premium.
