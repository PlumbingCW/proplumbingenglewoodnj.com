A leak in a tower is a coordination problem

In a house, you close the main and the leak stops. In a high-rise, the water may come from a pipe inside another unit, a shared stack, a riser or a rooftop line, and shutting it off can affect dozens of homes. A plumber therefore needs the building manager, co-op board or landlord on the phone early.

If you are a tenant or unit owner, call management and then call us right away, since both calls matter. For a burst line, our burst pipe repair page explains what a plumber does first. Give them the unit number and the location of the stain. That one call often gets the riser valve closed and the unit above entered, which stops the damage faster than anything you can do inside your own apartment.

Risers, shared stacks and the unit below you

A riser is the vertical supply pipe that feeds units on each floor, and a stack is the vertical drain pipe that carries waste down. Buildings here range from the 1960s to the 2000s, so ask the engineer what material and age your risers are. A pinhole leak in an old riser can soak several floors before anyone sees it.

About 7.1 percent of Fort Lee's housing units were vacant in 2020. A leak in an empty unit can run for days, so building staff should walk vacant apartments after a cold snap or after a nearby leak report.

Before we arrive: burst pipe or active leak in Fort Lee

  1. 1

    Shut your unit valve

    Look under sinks, behind toilets and near the water heater for fixture valves. Many apartments also have a unit shutoff in a closet or utility room.

  2. 2

    Call the front desk or super

    Management can close a riser, enter the unit above and give a plumber elevator or loading dock access.

  3. 3

    Protect electronics and floors

    Move devices, rugs and wall hangings out of the path. Put towels at doorways to slow water heading into hallways.

  4. 4

    Tell neighbors below

    Knock on the door of the unit under yours. A leak can reach two or three floors in an hour.

  5. 5

    Phone the 24/7 line

    Give the building name, floor and unit, and whether management has been reached. If you smell gas, leave and call 911 or the gas utility.

Height, pressure and the building's pumps

Water pressure at the top of a tall building does not come from the street alone. Many buildings use booster pumps and pressure-reducing valves to keep the top and bottom floors balanced. If your faucets suddenly sputter or surge, the cause may be in the mechanical room rather than in your unit.

Report it to the building engineer first. We can then work on fixtures, branch lines and heaters inside the unit, or coordinate with the engineer on the shared equipment.
