No heat, a leak or a safety scare

If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, leave the building at once with everyone inside, then call 911. Do not stay to look for the cause and do not go back in. A gas smell works the same way: get out and call the gas utility or 911 first.

For a no heat call without any alarm, check the simple things first, then call. If the boiler is leaking steadily, switch off its power at the emergency switch and close the water supply to it, but only if you can reach both without standing in water.

  • 1Confirm the thermostat is on heat, set above room temperature and has working batteries
  • 2Look at the boiler for a tripped breaker, a lit display or a lockout light
  • 3Press a reset button once only if the manual allows it, never repeatedly
  • 4If pipes may be frozen, keep the heat on and call us before anything splits
  • 5Keep children and pets away from hot pipes and any wet floor
  • 6Do not remove covers, bypass safety switches or add water to a hot steam boiler

Steam and hot water boilers are different animals

A hot water boiler heats water and pushes it through pipes to radiators or baseboards with a circulator pump, then it returns to be heated again. A steam boiler boils water into steam that rises through pipes to radiators and condenses back to water. Steam systems rely on correct water level, pitched piping and working vents.

That difference shapes the repair. Hot water systems tend to show trouble through pressure, air, zone valves or pumps. Steam systems more often show trouble through water level, banging pipes and radiator vents that stop working.

Many houses with older radiators run on one type or the other, and it is not always obvious. A look at the boiler tag and the pressure gauge or sight glass settles it quickly.

Thermostat trouble or boiler trouble

Many no heat calls turn out to be controls rather than the boiler. A dead thermostat battery, a miswired replacement or a zone valve that stays closed will leave radiators cold while the boiler looks fine.

We separate the two by watching whether the thermostat calls for heat, whether the boiler responds and whether the circulator moves water. Frozen pipes sometimes accompany a cold house, and if one has split, our burst pipe service covers the repair. A water heater repair visit may also apply where your hot water is heated separately.

What affects the price of boiler work

A bad circulator and a failed heat exchanger are very different bills, so the diagnosis steers everything.

The failed part

Pumps, valves, gauges and controls cost far less than a boiler body or heat exchanger.

System type and age

Steam and older cast iron boilers can need specialised parts and more time.

Access

A boiler in a crowded basement corner takes longer to service.

Hours

A night or weekend callout may cost more than a daytime visit.

Repair versus replace

When repairs pile up on an old boiler, replacement may cost less over time.

Insurance may cover sudden damage such as a burst pipe from a frozen system, but wear, corrosion and normal breakdown of the boiler itself are generally not covered. A home warranty or service contract is a separate matter, so ask your carrier and read any contract you hold.

Annual service keeps boilers running in January

A yearly visit before heating season checks controls, safety devices, water level or pressure, venting and leaks, and cleans or flushes parts that collect scale. It is the cheapest way to avoid a Saturday night failure in a cold snap.

Combustion testing and burner cleaning are heating trade work, and we will say so if your system needs it. We scope what falls to a plumber, such as valves, piping, expansion tanks and pumps, and help you line up the rest.

High pressure often points to a waterlogged expansion tank or a fill valve that leaks by. Low pressure points to a small leak somewhere in the loop. Both need diagnosis because adding water to a leaking system only hides the problem.

In hot water systems, gurgling is usually trapped air. In steam systems, banging often comes from water pooled in sagging pipes. Kettling, a rumbling from the boiler itself, suggests scale on the heat exchanger.

Water near electrical controls is a hazard, so turn off power at the boiler switch if it is safe to reach. A slow drip from a valve is common and fixable. A leak from the boiler body usually means it is near the end of its life.
