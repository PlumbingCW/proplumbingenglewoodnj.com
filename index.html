Water coming through the ceiling? Do this first

These four steps limit damage while you wait for help.

  1. Shut off the main water valve. It is usually where the line enters the house, often near the meter.
  2. Stay clear of electricity. If water is near outlets, a panel or appliances, do not step into it. Call first.
  3. Photograph the damage for your insurance company before you move anything.
  4. Call the 24/7 line and tell dispatch where the water or sewage is coming from.

Smell gas? Leave the building and call the gas utility or 911 before you call a plumber.

Emergency plumber in Englewood: what the 24/7 line covers

An emergency plumber in Englewood, NJ is a licensed plumber who answers when water, sewage or lost heat will not wait until Monday. The line is open 24/7, including nights, weekends and holidays, and a call is answered whether the problem started at noon or at 3 a.m.

The calls that need fast help are burst or leaking pipes, sewage backing up into a tub or floor drain, a water heater leaking onto the floor, a toilet that will not stop overflowing, and a boiler that quits in January. A sump pump that fails during a storm belongs on that list too, and so does a kitchen faucet valve that will not shut off.

Plan on the dispatcher asking four things: the address, what you see, whether you have found the main shutoff, and whether anyone is in danger. Those answers decide what kind of plumber is sent and what to bring. Same-day appointments are offered when available, and you hear a clear price before work starts.

Not every problem is an emergency. A dripping faucet, a slow bathroom sink or a running toilet can wait for a scheduled visit, and the table below helps you tell the difference before you call.

Emergency line or scheduled visit?

Use this as a quick guide. If you are unsure, call and describe the problem, because a dispatcher can usually tell in a minute.

What Englewood homeowners should know about local plumbing

Englewood is a city of 29,308 residents in Bergen County, and it has been settled for a long time. Passenger rail service reached the city in 1859, so many streets near Palisade Avenue and Engle Street hold older houses. Older homes can still carry original cast iron drains or galvanized supply lines, so ask a plumber to identify your pipe material before you decide between a repair and a bigger project.

Bergen County's Oradell Reservoir system supplies a large part of northern Bergen County with drinking water, and Veolia treats that water at its Haworth plant. Check the supplier named on your water bill and ask that supplier for its annual water quality report. That report is the right source for questions about scale on a tankless heater or spots on glassware.

Plumbing in New Jersey must be performed by a licensed master plumber, so ask any plumber for a licence number and confirm it with the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers. For permit questions, call the Englewood Building & Code Enforcement office at 2-10 North Van Brunt Street. Permit rules vary by job, so ask before you start.
