Installation matters just as much as repair. A pit that is too shallow, a pump that is oversized, or a discharge pipe that dumps water beside the foundation can undo a good pump within a season. We size the pump to the pit and to how the water actually arrives, then set the piping so it stays out of the way of the float.

Basement bathrooms and laundry rooms below the sewer line use a different machine, called an ejector pump, and we install and repair those as well.

How a sump pump works and where it usually fails

A sump pit is a lined hole, often 18 to 24 inches wide, cut into the basement slab. Groundwater or drain tile water runs into it, a float rises with the water, and the float switch starts the pump. The pump pushes water up a discharge pipe, through a check valve and out of the house.

Most failures come from a handful of parts. The float switch can snag on the pit wall or a power cord and never lift. Silt and gravel can jam the impeller. A worn motor may hum but not spin. A cheap check valve can stick open, which lets the water in the pipe drop back into the pit and makes the pump cycle over and over.

A pump that runs constantly is not always broken. It may be losing a fight with a high water table or a clogged discharge line, so we trace the whole path before recommending a new unit.

Sump pump stopped and the pit is filling

If water is rising toward the top of the pit, you have a short window. Your goals are to keep electrical hazards away, slow the water if you safely can, and protect what is stored low. Call us at once and tell us whether the pump hums, runs or sits silent.

Never reach into a pit while the pump is plugged in, and never stand in water near live outlets. If the basement already has standing water around the panel or outlets, stay upstairs and call the electric utility or 911 if you see sparking.

  • 2If the floor is dry and it is safe, look at whether the float moves freely without touching the pit wall

  • 4Take photos of any water and damaged items before you move them, for your insurance records

Battery backup, discharge lines and check valves

Storms that fill pits are often the same storms that knock out power, which leaves a plug-in pump with nothing to run on. A battery backup pump sits beside the main pump on its own float and runs off a deep cycle battery with a charger. It buys hours, not days, and the battery needs testing and eventual replacement.

The discharge line deserves equal attention. It should carry water well away from the foundation, slope so it drains between cycles, and have a way out if one end freezes in January. Where it can be done, a short outlet above grade lets water escape if a buried section ices over.

Ask about the check valve whenever a pump is replaced. It is a small part that fails often, and a quiet, well-seated valve keeps the pump from cycling needlessly.

Ejector pumps for basement bathrooms and laundry

A basement toilet, shower or laundry sink sits lower than the main sewer line, so gravity cannot carry the waste away. An ejector pump collects it in a sealed, vented pit and lifts it, solids included, up to the house drain. It is a different machine from a sump pump and should never share a pit with groundwater.

Ejector pumps fail for the same reasons as sump pumps and for a few more. Wipes and hygiene products jam them, and a failed lid seal lets sewer gas into the room. If a basement drain is backing up as well, a drain cleaning visit may be part of the answer.

What affects the price of sump pump work

A pump swap in an existing pit costs far less than a new pit, and several details decide where yours lands.

Repair or replacement

A new float switch or check valve costs much less than a new pump, so we test the pump first.

Pump type and horsepower

Pedestal pumps, submersible pumps and sewage ejectors differ in build and price, and the right size depends on inflow.

Pit condition

A cracked, silted or undersized pit may need work before any pump will last.

Discharge routing

A long run, a buried section or a frozen line adds pipe and labour.

Backup power

A battery backup adds a second pump, a battery, a charger and an alarm.

Insurance may respond to sudden and accidental water damage, but many policies exclude groundwater and sump overflow unless you added a water backup endorsement. Wear, age and lack of maintenance are usually not covered, so ask your carrier before you need it.
