Commercial plumbing in Englewood, NJ covers repairs, maintenance and emergency calls for the plumbing in businesses and multi-unit buildings, from a restaurant kitchen to an office restroom to an apartment building riser. If water is flowing and you can reach the shutoff, close it and call our after-hours line. We talk the job through on the phone first and tell you honestly whether it fits.

Commercial systems differ from houses. Fixtures serve many people, pipes are larger, and a failure can close a business for the day. Most calls involve drains, leaks, water heaters or fixtures, but some work, such as fire sprinkler lines and large mechanical systems, belongs to specialty contractors.

This page covers common commercial calls, how they are handled and where a different contractor is the right choice. Landlords and property managers will find their questions near the end.

Grease traps, interceptors and kitchen drains

A grease trap or interceptor catches fats, oils and grease before they enter the sewer. It only works when it is emptied and cleaned on a regular basis. When it is full, grease passes through and builds up in the lateral, which is how a restaurant ends up with a backed-up kitchen line.

Cleaning means pumping out the contents, scraping the walls and baffles, and checking the inlet and outlet. Larger outdoor interceptors are often serviced by a licensed waste hauler, and we will tell you on the phone if yours falls into that group. A kitchen line that still backs up after trap service often needs hydro jetting to strip the grease from the pipe.

Backflow tests, floor drains and storm drains

Backflow prevention devices keep dirty water from flowing back into the drinking supply. Many commercial buildings have them on irrigation, fire lines, boilers or kitchen equipment. Testing is done by a certified tester, and your water supplier, named on your bill, can say whether your device needs periodic testing.

A floor drain that overflows in a boiler room, a garage or a kitchen often signals a blocked branch or a main line that cannot carry the load. Do not assume the drain alone is at fault. Cleaning it and running a camera down the line shows where the real restriction sits.

Storm drain cleaning keeps parking lot and roof drains flowing before heavy rain. Leaves, grit and trash settle in the basin and the line below it. Clearing them ahead of a storm is easier than cleaning up a flooded loading dock afterward.

Commercial leak detection and where it stops

In a small shop or office, leak detection works the same way as in a house. Zones are shut off, the meter is watched, and sections are pressure tested until the source is narrowed down. A wet wall in a clinic or a climbing water bill in a row of offices can usually be traced this way.

Large buildings are different. Pressurized fire lines, multi-story risers and buried mains serving several buildings can need specialty contractors and equipment. Rather than guess, we scope the job on the phone first and say plainly if we are the right fit. If we are not, we would rather tell you than send a plumber who cannot finish the job.

Business plumbing failures after hours

A burst pipe, a sewage backup or a dead water heater does not wait for Monday. Our line is open 24/7 for business calls too, and the first step is a phone conversation about the building, the system and the symptom. Tell us whether you own the space, rent it or manage it, since that affects who can approve the work.

If the problem sits in a shared system, such as a riser or a main drain serving other tenants, contact the building manager or landlord as well. They may need to authorize work and shut water to other units.

  • 1Locate and close the nearest shutoff valve, or the main if a pipe is spraying
  • 2Keep customers and staff out of wet areas and away from electrical equipment
  • 3Cover or move inventory, records and equipment before the water spreads
  • 4Stop using restrooms and kitchen drains if sewage is backing up
  • 5Call the landlord or property manager if you lease the space
  • 6Photograph the damage and note the time for insurance

What affects the price of commercial plumbing work

Business jobs vary more than household ones, and these are the main drivers.

Building size and system

A single restroom is simpler than a multi-floor building with several risers.

Access and hours

Working after hours or around opening times affects scheduling and rates, and you will hear the price before work starts.

Fixtures and equipment

Commercial toilets, kitchen equipment and larger water heaters use different parts and take more labor than household units.

Permits and inspections

Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Englewood Building & Code Enforcement office. Commercial work can involve more steps than a house repair.

Specialty scope

Fire lines, large mechanical systems and hauled grease waste are separate trades, billed by those contractors.

Sudden accidental discharge may be covered under a commercial property policy, while gradual leaks, wear and maintenance usually are not. Tenants and landlords may carry different policies, so ask your carrier and read your lease.
