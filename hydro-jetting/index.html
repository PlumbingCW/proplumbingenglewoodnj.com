Emergency Hydro Jetting in Englewood, NJ

High-pressure water cleaning for greasy, rooted or scaled drain and sewer lines, with a camera look first so the pressure fits the pipe.

Call (551) 249-3045

Hydro jetting in Englewood, NJ means cleaning the inside of a drain or sewer line with a high-pressure stream of water, so the pipe wall is scrubbed instead of just poked through. If sewage or gray water is coming up a floor drain or tub right now, stop running water in the house and call. Our line is open 24/7, and a licensed plumber can tell you on the phone whether this is a jetting job.

A jetter feeds a flexible hose with a special nozzle into a cleanout or drain opening. Jets on the nozzle point backward and pull the hose along, while water at pressure, often several thousand psi on residential equipment, strips grease, sludge, sand and fine roots off the pipe wall. A cable snake can punch a hole through a blockage and leave a coating of the same material on the wall. That coating is why some clogs return within weeks.

Not every pipe suits jetting. Fragile or collapsed lines can be damaged by pressure, which is why a camera look comes before the hose goes in. The sections below explain when jetting beats a cable, what moves the price, and when it should not be used. A single slow sink usually needs plain drain cleaning instead.

Water coming back up your drains right now

A backup that reaches the floor drain, shower or basement toilet is a sewage problem, not a nuisance. Raw sewage carries bacteria, so keep children and pets away and do not wade through it barefoot. Jetting is often the right fix when the line has backed up more than once, because repeated backups point to buildup that a simple snake leaves behind.

When you call, say which fixtures are affected. If every drain is slow, or the lowest ones gurgle when upstairs water runs, the blockage is probably in the main line and not in a single branch.

  • 1Stop using sinks, tubs, washers and toilets until the line is open, since every gallon goes to the same blocked pipe
  • 2Skip chemical drain cleaner on a backed-up line. It sits in the pipe and can burn whoever works on it next
  • 3Stay out of a flooded basement if outlets, the panel or appliances are in the water, and call an electrician if the panel is wet
  • 4Wear boots and gloves for cleanup, and bag soaked rugs instead of carrying them through the house
  • 5Note when it started and which fixture backed up first, so the plumber can narrow the location
  • 6Clear the path to the cleanout, usually a capped pipe near the foundation or in the basement

A jetter scrubs the pipe, a snake drills a hole

A cable machine, often called a snake, spins a steel cable with a cutting head. It is good at breaking through a hard stoppage such as a wad of wipes or a clump of roots. What it leaves is a channel about as wide as the cutter, with the rest of the buildup still clinging to the pipe wall.

Jetting works the full circumference of the pipe. As the hose moves forward and back, the water strips grease rings, mineral scale and sludge, then flushes the debris downstream to the public sewer. In a 4-inch house sewer, that can restore close to the full bore, which is the goal when water drains slowly but not fully stopped.

Jetting also needs a clear path for the water to leave. If the line is blocked solid downstream, the plumber opens it first, usually through the cleanout, before turning up the pressure.

Root intrusion, grease and scale, one at a time

Roots enter through joints and small cracks looking for moisture. Once inside they grow into a fibrous mat that catches paper and grease. A jetter with a cutting nozzle can clear that mat, but the roots regrow from outside the pipe unless the joint is sealed. If the camera shows an open joint, plan on a repeat visit or a repair.

Kitchen grease cools as it travels, clings to the pipe and hardens into a layer that traps more. Over time a 4-inch pipe can shrink to a narrow channel. Jetting strips these layers far better than a blade can, and a follow-up cleaning schedule keeps them from rebuilding.

Older cast iron develops rough scale on its inner wall, which snags waste and slows flow. Jetting can knock loose the soft scale, but thin, rusted iron can be fragile. That is another reason the camera goes in first.

Signs a cable alone will not hold

If more than one of these sounds familiar, snaking is probably only buying time.

What a jetting visit involves

  1. 01

    Find the way in

    The plumber locates the cleanout, or pulls a toilet if there is none, and checks which way the line runs.

  2. 02

    Look before pressure

    A camera on a flexible rod shows grease, roots, sags or breaks, so the pressure is matched to the condition of the pipe.

  3. 03

    Jet the line

    The hose goes in with the nozzle running, working past the blockage and back out in passes until the water moves freely.

  4. 04

    Check the result

    A second camera pass or a flow test confirms the line is open, and you hear about anything that needs repair.

What affects the price of hydro jetting

Jetting is priced by the job, and these are the things that move the number.

Length and size of the line

A short run from a cleanout to the street takes less time than a long lateral with several bends. Pipe diameter changes the nozzle and the technique.

How heavy the buildup is

Light grease may take one pass. Packed roots, scale or years of sludge take repeated passes, more water and more time.

Access to the line

A good exterior cleanout saves time. If the plumber has to remove a toilet to get in, the job takes longer.

Camera inspection

Looking first adds time but keeps a fragile pipe from being jetted. It also gives you a record of what the pipe looks like.

Hour of the call

After-hours backups are priced differently from scheduled work, and you will hear the price before work starts.

Sudden accidental discharge from a covered cause may be covered under some homeowners policies, but gradual buildup, wear and maintenance usually are not. Many policies treat sewer backup as an optional add-on, so ask your carrier before the job.
