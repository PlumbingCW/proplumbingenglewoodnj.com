Sewer Inspection in Englewood, NJ

A video look inside your sewer lateral before you buy, line, dig or keep paying for repeat backups.

Call (551) 249-3045

A sewer inspection in Englewood, NJ is a video examination of the pipe that carries waste from your house to the public sewer, done with a small waterproof camera on a flexible rod. That pipe is called the sewer lateral, and the part on your property is usually the owner's to maintain. Unlike an urgent backup, an inspection is a planned visit, and same-day appointments are available when the schedule allows.

People book an inspection for three common reasons. They are buying or selling a house, they have had a backup and want to know why, or a plumber has recommended lining or replacement and they want to see the proof. Each reason changes what you ask the camera to look for.

If a line fails or backs up while a project is underway, our phone line is open 24/7. The rest of this page covers what the camera shows, how to read the footage and what to do with the result.

The sewer lateral, in plain terms

The lateral runs from the drain at the foundation out to the public sewer main, usually under the yard and sometimes under the street. It is typically 4 or 6 inches across and may be clay, cast iron, fiber, PVC or ABS, or a mix. Many houses have a cleanout, a capped pipe that gives a camera a place to enter.

The lateral is easy to forget because nothing shows above ground. Defects develop slowly, so a line that works today can have root-filled joints or a sag that holds water. The camera is the only way to see these without digging.

How the camera visit runs

  1. 01

    Choose the entry point

    The plumber finds the cleanout, or pulls a toilet if the house has none.

  2. 02

    Run the camera downstream

    A push camera on a flexible cable travels toward the street while it records, and a transmitter in the camera head lets the plumber trace its position above ground.

  3. 03

    Mark the problems

    Each defect is noted by its distance from the entry point and, when the locator is used, marked on the ground above.

  4. 04

    Review the footage together

    You see the video, hear which findings are minor and which are urgent, and can ask for a copy of the recording.

What the picture can and cannot show

A camera shows pipe material, joint spacing, roots, cracks, offsets, scale, grease and standing water. It shows whether the line is round or crushed and whether a belly holds water. It also shows where branch lines enter and whether the lateral connects to the street main.

It does not show everything. A camera cannot see through a full blockage, so the line may need cleaning first. It cannot always see the public main past the connection, and it will not tell you how many more years a pipe has. Treat the footage as a snapshot, not a warranty.

Reading the report: terms you will hear

Inspection notes use a short list of words, and each one points toward a different next step.

  • ✓Root intrusion means roots have entered at a joint or crack
  • ✓An offset joint means two pipe sections no longer line up, leaving a ledge that catches paper
  • ✓A belly or sag is a low spot where water pools and waste settles
  • ✓Scale is rough rust buildup, common inside old cast iron
  • ✓A fracture is a break along the pipe wall, sometimes with soil visible through it
  • ✓A collapse is a crushed or separated section, which usually needs excavation
  • ✓A tap or connection is where a branch line joins the main, often a weak point

Buying a house with an unseen lateral

A general home inspection usually does not cover the buried sewer line, since the inspector cannot see it. A separate camera scope before closing gives you facts to use in negotiation. If the footage shows roots or a collapsed section, you can ask for a repair credit or a seller repair, or at least budget for the work.

Schedule the camera visit within your inspection contingency period, and ask the seller for access to the cleanout. Bring the footage to your attorney or agent. In an older house, expect to find some defects, so focus on whether they are minor or structural.

Camera first, lining second

A liner is only as good as the pipe it goes into. Before lining, a camera shows whether the pipe is round, whether any section has separated, and whether a belly will hold water after the liner cures. A cleaning pass usually comes first, because the liner needs a clean wall.

If the footage shows a collapse or a deep sag, lining is the wrong tool and the plumber should say so. Then the next page to read is sewer line repair, which covers spot repairs and replacement. If the lateral is simply dirty, hydro jetting may be all it needs.

What affects the price of a sewer inspection

A camera visit is usually a smaller job than a repair, and these factors set the price.

Access

A working cleanout allows quick entry, while pulling a toilet or locating a buried line adds time.

Line length and bends

Long laterals and extra turns take longer to run.

Cleaning first

If the camera cannot pass a blockage or heavy grease, the line must be cleaned first, which is a separate service.

Locating and marking

Tracing the line above ground and marking defect depth adds time but helps if a repair follows.

Report and recording

A written summary and a copy of the video may be priced separately or included, so ask before the visit.

An inspection is an information and maintenance expense and is usually not covered by homeowners insurance. If a sudden accidental discharge caused damage, ask your carrier whether they want to see the footage.

Englewood houses, records and who to ask

Englewood has 11,563 housing units in a city incorporated in 1899, with rail service arriving in 1859. Houses went up across many decades, and some laterals have been patched, rerouted or replaced along the way. Ask the seller for records of past sewer work in this part of Bergen County, then compare them with what the camera shows.

The Englewood Building & Code Enforcement office, at 2-10 North Van Brunt Street, handles permits, so it is the place to ask whether any past plumbing work at an address was permitted. What is on file for a given address is for the office to say, so call before you rely on it.

The camera result decides what happens next, whether that is sewer line cleaning for roots and grease or sewer line repair for a cracked pipe. The same visit is available for homes in Leonia and the other nearby towns.

Check a plumber before you hire

New Jersey law says plumbing work must be done by a licensed master plumber. Ask for the licence number, then confirm it with the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers, part of the New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs.

Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Englewood Building & Code Enforcement office before work begins.
