A disposal that hums but will not spin, or one that leaks into the cabinet, can shut down a kitchen quickly. Garbage disposal repair in Englewood, NJ means locating the fault, whether a jam, a worn seal, a bad hose connection or a failed motor, and repairing it or installing a replacement unit. The line is open 24/7 for leaks that will not stop.

If the disposal is leaking, switch it off at the wall switch and the breaker, close the two stops under the sink if they work, and set a bucket below. Never put a hand into the grinding chamber, even with the power off. Use a wooden spoon handle to move anything you can see.

Disposal jams and humming motors

A humming sound with no spinning means the flywheel is stuck. Fibrous scraps, a bone, a bottle cap or a spoon can all cause it. With the power off at the switch and the breaker, many disposals have a hex socket in the center of the bottom. A 1/4-inch hex wrench turned back and forth frees the plate.

After that, press the red reset button on the bottom. If the unit trips again right away, the motor may have worn out or the overload is failing. A disposal that jams every week is telling you something about its age.

Where the leak is coming from

A disposal has three or four places that can leak, and each has its own fix.

Leak locationLikely causeWhat a plumber usually does
Under the sink flange at the topLoose mounting ring or failed gasket or puttyReset the flange with fresh sealant and tighten the mounting
Side drain outletCracked or loose discharge gasketReplace the gasket and clamp, check the drain line
Dishwasher hose connectionLoose hose clamp or cracked hoseReplace the hose or clamp and test a dishwasher cycle
Body of the unit or the bottomRusted or cracked housing, failed internal sealReplace the disposal, since the housing cannot be repaired
Air gap on the sink deckBlocked line downstream or clogged air gapClear the drain and clean the air gap
P-trap and pipes belowWorn washers or loose slip nutsRe-seat or replace the trap parts

Wipe every joint dry and run water for a minute. The new drip usually shows you which joint is at fault.

Dishwasher hoses and the air gap

The dishwasher drains through a hose that connects to a small nipple on the disposal. When a new disposal is installed, there is a plastic knockout plug inside that nipple that must be removed. If it is left in place, the dishwasher cannot drain, and water stands in the bottom of the tub.

Some sinks also have an air gap, a small chrome or plastic cylinder on the sink deck. It keeps dirty sink water from running back into the dishwasher. If water spits from the air gap, the drain line past the disposal is probably partially blocked. Some homes use a high loop of hose instead, so ask the Englewood Building & Code Enforcement office which setup applies at your address.

Repair or replace, what to weigh

A short list helps decide whether to spend on a repair or put the money toward a new unit.

  • ✓Age of the unit, since most disposals are due for replacement after many years of service
  • ✓Rust or moisture at the bottom of the housing
  • ✓Repeated jams or a motor that stalls under a light load
  • ✓Whether the horsepower suits how much you cook, usually 1/3 to 3/4 horsepower for homes
  • ✓Whether the sink flange and drain pipes are in good shape
  • ✓Noise and vibration that have grown over time

When a disposal leak is urgent

A disposal leak that runs into a cabinet and onto the floor can swell wood and spread to the room below. Water coming from the body of the unit will not fix itself, so treat that as urgent. Smoke, a burning smell or sparks mean you should turn off the breaker and call.

A grinding chamber full of standing water that will not drain is also a reason for a prompt visit, since the drain line may be blocked.

  • 1Switch off the disposal and the breaker before anything else
  • 2Close the stops under the sink, or the main valve if they will not close
  • 3Empty the cabinet so you can see where the water starts
  • 4Do not use the sink or the dishwasher until the leak is found
  • 5Stay out of the cabinet if water has reached outlets or wiring

Installing a new disposal

Installation starts with removing the old unit and its mounting assembly. The new sink flange is seated in the drain opening with sealant or a gasket, and the disposal locks onto the mounting ring below. Then the discharge pipe, trap and dishwasher hose go back on, and the unit is run with water to test every joint.

Power is its own question. Some disposals plug into an outlet under the sink, and others are hardwired. Electrical work near water is not a DIY job, and the connection may need a licensed electrician. If the sink is being replaced too, faucet and sink plumbing often fits into the same visit.

What affects the price of garbage disposal repair

A reset and a full replacement are far apart, so the price depends on what the plumber finds.

Repair or replacement

A new gasket or hose is quick. Replacing a rusted unit requires removing the old one and fitting a new unit.

Unit size and features

Horsepower, noise insulation and feed type change the price of the disposal itself.

Drain line condition

If the trap or drain pipe beyond the disposal is blocked, clearing it is an added step.

Electrical connection

A missing outlet or a hardwire change may need a licensed electrician.

Cabinet and sink fit

A mismatched flange or a tight cabinet takes longer to work in.

A sudden leak that damages a cabinet or floor may be covered under homeowners insurance. A slow leak from an old, worn unit is typically treated as maintenance, so ask your carrier.
