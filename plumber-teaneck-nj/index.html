Calling for an emergency plumber in Teaneck, NJ? An emergency plumber is a licensed plumber who responds to burst pipes, sewage backups, flooding and failed water heaters at any hour, and our Englewood office is about 3 miles from the township line.

Teaneck is large. The 2020 Census counted 41,246 residents on 6.24 square miles, making it the second most populous of Bergen County's 70 municipalities. Dense housing means a lot of shared walls, shared sewer mains and old plumbing, so a few problems account for most calls.

This page starts with those problems and then explains how Teaneck's roads, creek and institutions shape the visit.

The calls that fill the board in a township this size

Three kinds of call come up most. Basement water is first, because parts of the township sit on low ground near the Hackensack River and Overpeck County Park. Slow or backed-up drains come second, especially where a single sewer lateral serves a tub, a washer and a kitchen sink. Water heater failures come third, since tanks give out with little warning.

In a place this compact, a backup in one house can be a symptom of a blockage down the street. If your neighbors report the same gurgle, it is worth asking whether the issue is in the lateral or the municipal main. A camera look through your cleanout answers that, and our guide to drain cleaning explains what the plumber will do first.

Teaneck quick facts

One ZIP, 07666

All of Teaneck uses ZIP 07666. West Englewood and New Bridge are unincorporated areas partly or fully within the township, so say which one you mean.

Four directions to learn

Route 4 splits the township into north and south, and the CSX River Subdivision splits it east and west. Tell dispatch which quarter you are in.

Around the clock

Emergency dispatch is available around the clock for homes, apartments and commercial properties. Non-urgent work becomes a same-day appointment when available.

Higher than Englewood

Teaneck's elevation is 128 feet, compared with 43 feet in Englewood, but low pockets near the creek and river still collect water.

Low ground by the creek and Overpeck Park

Teaneck Creek Conservancy, established in 2001, has 1.3 miles of trails near where I-80 meets I-95. Overpeck County Park covers more than 800 acres, with about 500 donated by Teaneck, and the Hackensack River Greenway adds 3.5 miles along the water. Low ground next to creeks and floodplain parks tends to hold water after storms, so houses nearby should expect damp basements before houses on higher streets.

If you live near any of these, check that your sump pump discharges away from the foundation and test it before heavy rain. A pump that fails is worth fixing through sump pump installation and repair, and a battery backup is a reasonable question to ask during that visit.

Before we arrive: burst pipe or active leak in Teaneck

  1. 1

    Stop the water at the source

    Shut the fixture valve first if you can find it. If not, close the main where the line enters, then open one faucet to relieve pressure.

  2. 2

    Mind the basement

    Do not wade into a flooded basement if the panel, furnace or outlets are underwater. Wait for the electric utility or a plumber's all-clear.

  3. 3

    Tell neighbors in a shared building

    In a two-family house or apartment building, alert the unit below and the landlord right away. Water spreads through floors quickly.

    Tell dispatch your cross street, which side of Route 4 you are on, and which side of the railroad.

    Describe what you see and what you have shut off. A gas smell means everyone leaves, then call the gas utility or 911.

Which Teaneck are you in?

Teaneck borders Englewood and Leonia on the east, Bergenfield and New Milford to the north, Ridgefield Park and Bogota to the south, and River Edge and Hackensack across the Hackensack River to the west. Rush hour traffic on those roads can decide which route a crew takes, so a precise cross street helps more than a general neighborhood name.

Calls from Bogota and Hackensack just over the line come to the same dispatch.

Hospitals, a university and commercial blocks

Holy Name Medical Center and the Metropolitan Campus of Fairleigh Dickinson University anchor the township's institutional side, and the shops and restaurants around them depend on working restrooms and kitchen lines. For a business, even a few hours without water can mean closing, so commercial plumbing service should include a plan for after-hours access and who holds the keys.

Business owners nearby should know where their own shutoff is and keep it unblocked.

Check a plumber before you hire

New Jersey law says plumbing work must be done by a licensed master plumber. Ask for the licence number, then confirm it with the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers, part of the New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs.
