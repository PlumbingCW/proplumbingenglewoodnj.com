East Hill is the part of Englewood, NJ where, according to the city's Master Plan, most of the larger homes were built. The plan describes it as running roughly from Booth Street to Linden Street, and from Grand Avenue and Engle Street over to North and South Woodland. If your house is in that area, this page covers the plumbing questions that come with a bigger home.

The same plan says these houses went up mainly in the first half of the 20th century, in Dutch Colonial Revival, Tudor and other revival styles, and it mentions estates built by Wall Street magnates. Those boundaries are the plan's own approximations. If you are unsure whether your street counts, just give the street name when you call.

Englewood's East Hill is a different place from East Hill in neighboring Tenafly. They sit in separate towns with separate building departments, and Tenafly has its own page for its side.

East Hill at a glance

Where it sits

East Hill lies on the east side of Englewood, which the Master Plan describes as largely large-lot, single-family neighborhoods. Grand Avenue and Engle Street, which carry apartments and businesses, are among the streets the plan uses to place it.

What the housing looks like

Larger houses built mainly in the first half of the 20th century, many in revival styles. The plan does not say anything about the pipes inside them, so each house needs its own inspection.

A historic label to handle carefully

The plan calls the area the East Hill Historic District. It also says the city's list of about 800 historic properties is honorary and offers no protection. Ask the building department before assuming a rule applies to your house.

Which office handles permits

East Hill is in Englewood, so permit questions go to Englewood, not to Tenafly. The office to call is the Englewood Building & Code Enforcement office at (201) 871-6642, open Monday to Friday, 9:00 a.m. to 5:00 p.m., excluding holidays.

A bigger house asks different plumbing questions

A larger house usually has more bathrooms, more fixtures and longer pipe runs than a small one. That is general plumbing, not a finding about East Hill. It matters because every extra fixture is one more place for a valve, a supply line or a drain to need attention.

It also changes how problems show up. A leak in an upper-floor bathroom can travel through a ceiling before anyone sees it, and a slow drain in one bathroom can mean something different from slow drains in three. Knowing which fixtures are affected is the first thing a plumber will ask.

We do not assume what is inside the walls of any particular house. Pipe material, age and condition vary from one address to the next, and only a look on site answers it.

Check a plumber before you hire

New Jersey law says plumbing work must be done by a licensed master plumber. Ask for the licence number, then confirm it with the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers, part of the New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs.
