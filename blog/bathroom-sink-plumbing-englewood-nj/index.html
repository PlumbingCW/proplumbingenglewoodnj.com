Plumbing a New Bathroom Sink in Englewood, NJ: Stops, Trap, Vent, and Permits

Plumbing a new bathroom sink means connecting two supply lines through shutoff valves, a drain through a P-trap, and a vent that lets that drain breathe.

By the Englewood Pro Plumbing teamPublished October 30, 2026Updated October 30, 20266 min read

Plumbing a new bathroom sink means connecting two supply lines through shutoff valves, a drain through a P-trap, and a vent that lets that drain breathe. If the new sink replaces an old one in the same spot, the existing stub-outs often work. If it moves to a new wall, a plumber has to rough in new supply, drain, and vent pipes.

In Englewood, NJ, a change of location is the point where permit questions begin. Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Englewood Building & Code Enforcement office before work starts.

Same spot or new spot

The location decides almost everything about cost and difficulty.

The more framing, slab, or tile the pipe has to cross, the faster this moves from a project into rough-in work.

Supply stops and the faucet hookup

Each fixture should have its own shutoff valve, called a supply stop, on the hot and cold lines. These let you shut off one sink without losing water to the house. Hot is on the left and cold on the right, as you face the sink.

Short flexible supply lines, usually 3/8 inch, run from each stop to the faucet, and a leaking connection there is a faucet repair job. Tighten the connection by hand and then a quarter turn with a wrench. Too much force cracks nuts and ruins the rubber seal.

Before you set the faucet, flush the lines for a minute with the aerator off. Grit from new work clogs aerators and scratches cartridges. Then open the stops slowly and check every joint with a dry tissue.

The P-trap, the drain arm, and slope

The drain starts at the sink's pop-up assembly and runs through a tailpiece to the P-trap, the U-shaped bend that holds water and blocks sewer gas. From the trap, a horizontal arm leads to the drain stub in the wall. Bathroom sink drains are commonly 1-1/4 or 1-1/2 inch.

The arm should slope gently toward the wall, a rule of thumb of about 1/4 inch per foot. Too steep and water can outrun the solids, too flat and it drains slowly. The stub height varies, and in many homes it sits roughly 18 to 22 inches above the floor, but follow what your framing allows.

Do not use an S-trap, a design that can siphon the water seal out. If your old setup used one, a plumber can replace it with a P-trap and a vent.

Putting a replacement sink in the same location

  1. 01

    Shut off and drain

    Close the stops, open the old faucet, and put a bucket under the trap.

  2. 02

    Remove the old fixture

    Disconnect the supply lines and trap, then cut through the old caulk and lift the sink.

  3. 03

    Inspect the stubs

    Look for corrosion, a leaking stop valve, or a drain stub that is crooked or damaged.

  4. 04

    Set the new sink and faucet

    Install the faucet on the sink before mounting it, which is easier than reaching under later.

  5. 05

    Connect and test

    Hook up supply lines, pop-up, and trap, then fill and drain the sink while watching every joint.

Why venting matters at the new sink

Every trap needs a vent so draining water doesn't pull the trap seal out and so the drain flows smoothly. A sink with no vent gurgles and may leak sewer gas into the room. Adding a sink to a new spot means finding the nearest vent or tying into the vent stack.

Some homes allow an air admittance valve in specific cases, but whether it is acceptable depends on local approval. That is a question for the licensed plumber and the building office, not something to guess about.

Look into plumbing installation work if the sink move needs new drain and vent piping inside a wall. The plumber will check what is in the wall before anything is cut.

When to call a plumber instead of doing it yourself

Stop and call if you notice any of these.

  • ✓The sink is moving to a different wall
  • ✓A shutoff valve will not close or drips when turned
  • ✓Pipes are galvanized steel, corroded, or hard to loosen
  • ✓The drain stub is damaged or missing
  • ✓You find no vent or an unexplained vent connection
  • ✓The job requires opening walls, the floor, or tile
