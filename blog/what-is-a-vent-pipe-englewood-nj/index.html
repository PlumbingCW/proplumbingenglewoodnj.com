What Is a Vent Pipe in Plumbing? An Englewood, NJ Homeowner Guide

A vent pipe is the pipe that runs from your drain system up through the roof so air can enter behind flowing water and sewer gas can escape outside.

Call (551) 249-3045
By the Englewood Pro Plumbing teamPublished October 30, 2026Updated October 30, 20267 min read

A vent pipe is the pipe that runs from your drain system up through the roof so air can enter behind flowing water and sewer gas can escape outside. Without it, draining water would pull a vacuum, drains would gurgle, and the water seal in each trap could be sucked out.

Most homes in Englewood, NJ have a few vent pipes that join into one or two stacks poking above the roofline. When one clogs, you notice slow drains, bubbling, and odors. A plumber can usually clear or replace a vent, but climbing onto the roof to do it is a job for professionals.

How air makes water drain

Think of pouring from a juice can with one hole punched in it. The liquid glugs and stalls because air cannot get in. A drain pipe works the same way. As a slug of water moves down, it needs air behind it, and the vent pipe supplies that air.

Vents also protect the trap. The curved P-trap under a sink holds a small plug of water that blocks sewer gas from entering the room. If suction from a draining pipe pulled that water out, the house would smell like the sewer. A correctly vented trap keeps its seal.

Finally, the vent gives sewer gas somewhere safe to go. Gas rises up the vent stack and disperses above the roof instead of lingering near your windows.

Symptoms that point to a blocked or missing vent

A vent problem often looks like a drain problem. The pattern across several fixtures is the clue.

One slow sink is almost always a clog. A pattern of gurgling and odors across the house is where vents become suspects.

What blocks a vent in the first place

Roof vents collect leaves, small nests, and in some cases a dead bird or squirrel. In cold weather, frost can build up inside the opening and narrow it, a problem sometimes called frost closure. New Jersey winters make that a real possibility on a small-diameter vent.

Inside the walls, vents can fail for other reasons. A previous remodel may have cut or capped a vent. A pipe may have been installed with poor slope or without a vent at all. These are the cases where a plumber adds or reroutes venting rather than just clearing it.

Some homes use an air admittance valve, a one-way valve that lets air into the pipe without a roof penetration. Whether one is acceptable for a particular fixture is a question for the licensed plumber and the local building office.

What a plumber does to find and fix the problem

  1. 01

    Test the pattern

    Running fixtures one at a time shows which ones gurgle or slow when others drain.

  2. 02

    Inspect the drain

    A camera or snake from a cleanout rules out a main line clog, which can mimic a vent problem.

  3. 03

    Check the vent from above

    A plumber with proper roof safety gear looks at the vent terminals and may run a hose or line through them.

  4. 04

    Clear or repair

    That can mean clearing debris, replacing a damaged section, or rerouting a vent that was cut during past work.

  5. 05

    Retest

    Fixtures are run again together to confirm the gurgling and slow draining are gone.

Why a vent is not a DIY roof job

Falls from roofs and ladders injure many homeowners every year, and a vent clog is rarely urgent enough to justify that risk. Roof pitch, wet shingles, leaves, and cold mornings make it worse. Poking a hose or a snake into a vent from a ladder is not a safe plan.

There is also a plumbing reason to leave it alone. Pushing a clog down the vent can lodge it deeper in the pipe, and a broken flashing around a vent pipe can turn a minor clog into a roof leak.

What you can do safely is stay on the ground. Note which fixtures misbehave, whether it happens in wet weather or freezing weather, and take a photo of the roof vents from the yard. That information shortens the diagnosis.

What to tell the plumber before the visit

A short list of observations speeds up the diagnosis.

  • ✓Which fixtures gurgle, and what is happening elsewhere when they do
  • ✓Whether the problem started after a remodel, a new fixture, or a storm
  • ✓Whether it is worse in freezing weather
  • ✓Any sewer smell, and where in the house you notice it
  • ✓Whether the main drain has backed up recently
  • ✓Where the cleanout is, if you know

What Englewood and Bergen County homeowners should know about vent pipes

Englewood is a long-settled city, with passenger rail here since 1859, so many houses have been remodeled more than once, and the same is true in Teaneck and Englewood Cliffs. Old work is where vents get cut, capped, or routed in odd ways, and adding or rerouting one falls under plumbing installation work. Ask any plumber to trace the vent path rather than assuming it matches the original plan.

Plumbing work in New Jersey must be done by a master plumber, and the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers lets you confirm a licence. Ask the plumber for the licence number and check it with the Board before anyone works on your roof or your walls. If a fix means adding a new vent, permit rules vary by job, so ask the Englewood Building & Code Enforcement office.
