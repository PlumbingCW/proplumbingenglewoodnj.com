Boiler Not Working in Winter: Checks Before You Call 24/7 Boiler Repair

If your boiler quits on a cold night, you can check four things in a few minutes: the thermostat, the power switch and breaker, the pressure gauge, and whether the fuel supply is on.

By the Englewood Pro Plumbing teamPublished October 30, 2026Updated October 30, 20267 min read

If your boiler quits on a cold night, you can check four things in a few minutes: the thermostat, the power switch and breaker, the pressure gauge, and whether the fuel supply is on. If none of those fix it, call a 24/7 boiler repair service rather than opening the boiler yourself.

If a carbon monoxide alarm sounds, leave the house and call 911 first. For a heating failure in Englewood, NJ, the other urgent worry is frozen pipes, so keep the house as warm as you can until help arrives.

Safety first: when to leave instead of troubleshoot

A carbon monoxide alarm means leave. Get everyone outside, including pets, and call 911 from outside. Do not reset the alarm and go back in.

A gas smell means the same thing. Leave the building and call the gas utility or 911. Do not flip switches, light anything, or look for the source. Water leaking from the boiler near electrical parts also calls for staying clear.

Five visible checks, in order

  1. 01

    Set the thermostat to heat and raise it

    Put it about five degrees above the room temperature. Replace the batteries if the display is blank or dim.

  2. 02

    Check the boiler power switch and breaker

    Most boilers have a wall switch that looks like a light switch near the unit. Make sure it is on, and check the breaker panel for a tripped breaker.

  3. 03

    Read the pressure gauge

    A typical home hot-water boiler sits somewhere around 12 to 15 psi when cold, though your manual has the number. A needle near zero suggests a leak or low fill. A needle in the red may mean a pressure problem.

  4. 04

    Confirm the fuel supply

    For oil, look at the tank gauge. For gas, check that other gas appliances work. Do not touch the gas line or adjust valves other than those the manual names.

  5. 05

    Look at the display or lights

    Many boilers show a code or a blinking light. Write down what you see. A technician can use it.

What you see and what it points to

This table connects each visible sign to a likely cause and a sensible next move.

Why a boiler quits when it is coldest

A boiler works hardest on the coldest nights, so weak parts tend to fail then. A worn ignitor, a dirty flame sensor, a failing circulator pump, or low water pressure may limp along in mild weather and give out when demand goes up.

Fuel is another reason. An oil tank that ran low, or a filter clogged with sludge, can stop the burner exactly when you need it. Gas supply interruptions are rarer, but a pilot or ignitor that fails will have the same effect.

None of this is a reason to panic. It is a reason to keep a service record, note the boiler's age and model, and schedule a tune-up before heating season rather than after the first cold snap.

Protect the pipes while you wait

When the heat stops, the real danger in a freezing house is the water in the pipes. Pipes in exterior walls, unheated garages, crawl spaces, and basements near the foundation freeze first. A frozen pipe can split, and the leak shows up when the ice thaws.

Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls so room air can reach the pipes. Let a faucet at the far end of the house drip slowly. Use space heaters only if they are rated and plugged straight into a wall outlet, set on a hard floor, and away from anything that burns.

If a pipe does burst, shut off the main water valve right away and call for burst pipe repair. Stay out of any flooded area if water has reached outlets or electrical equipment.

What to tell the dispatcher

Clear information speeds up the call and helps the technician bring the right parts.

  • ✓Boiler brand, approximate age, and fuel type (oil or gas)
  • ✓What the display or lights show, and any code
  • ✓The pressure gauge reading
  • ✓What you have already checked, and whether you reset it
  • ✓Whether you have a carbon monoxide alarm and whether it is quiet
  • ✓Whether any pipes are cold, frozen, or leaking

What a licensed plumber does at 2 a.m.

A licensed technician starts with safety, checking for carbon monoxide, fuel leaks, and water near electrical parts. Then they check the controls, the ignition sequence, water pressure, the circulator, and the vent path.

Many failures are quick, such as a flame sensor cleaning or a thermostat wire. Others, such as a cracked heat exchanger or a failed circulator on an old system, may call for a part that is not on the truck. The tech can restore heat temporarily when it is safe, then plan the repair for daylight.

Questions homeowners ask
