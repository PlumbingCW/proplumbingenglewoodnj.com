No Dig Sewer Line Repair in Englewood, NJ: What Works and What Doesn't

No dig sewer line repair in Englewood, NJ means fixing the pipe between your house and the sewer main without trenching the whole yard.

Call (551) 249-3045
By the Englewood Pro Plumbing teamPublished October 30, 2026Updated October 30, 20268 min read

No dig sewer line repair in Englewood, NJ means fixing the pipe between your house and the sewer main without trenching the whole yard. The two main methods are lining the old pipe from the inside and pulling a new pipe through the old path, as covered on our sewer line repair page. Both need only a couple of small access pits.

It works when the pipe is still roughly in line and has not fully collapsed. A camera inspection decides that, so the video comes before any promise about lining, bursting, or a spot repair. Some failures still need a dig, and an honest plumber will say so.

Why the camera comes before any quote

A sewer line is buried, so nobody can judge it by looking at the lawn. A licensed plumber feeds a small waterproof camera into the line through a cleanout and watches the pipe wall on a monitor. The camera cable is marked with footage, and a locator can find the camera head from above ground.

That gives you three facts you need before spending money. First, what the pipe is made of, such as clay tile, cast iron, Orangeburg fiber pipe, or plastic. Second, what is wrong with it, such as roots in the joints, a cracked wall, a sag, or a collapse. Third, how far from the house the problem sits.

Ask for the recording or a clear summary with footage marks. If a company quotes a large repair without showing you the inside of the pipe, get a second opinion. A basic sewer camera inspection costs far less than guessing wrong.

Lining, bursting, spot repair, and full replacement side by side

Each method fits a different kind of damage. Use this as a starting point, then let the camera footage settle it.

MethodBest forMain limit
Pipe lining (cured in place)Cracks, root intrusion at joints, small gaps, pipe that is still in lineCannot fix a collapse or a deep sag, and the pipe gets slightly narrower
Pipe burstingBadly cracked or deteriorated pipe that is still straight enough to followNeeds an entry pit and an exit pit, and nearby utilities must be located first
Spot repair (small dig)One failed section, such as a broken joint or a crushed stretchRest of the old pipe stays, so other weak spots can show up later
Open trench replacementCollapsed pipe, major sags, wrong slope, or pipe that was never connected rightMost disruption to lawn, driveway, and landscaping

A line with several different problems along its length often ends up as a mix, such as lining one run and digging one short section.

How lining works inside the old pipe

Lining starts with cleaning. The plumber clears roots and scale with a jetter or cutter so the liner can sit tight against the pipe wall. The camera goes in again to confirm the pipe is clean and the path is clear.

A flexible tube soaked in resin is then pulled or inverted into the pipe and inflated. Heat, steam, or light cures the resin until it hardens into a new pipe inside the old one. Once cured, a cutter reopens the side connections, such as the line from a second bathroom group.

The liner is smooth and has no joints for roots to enter. The trade-off is a small loss in inside diameter, which rarely matters for a house line. Lining does not fix a pipe that has dropped out of alignment or holds water in a sag, because the liner follows the shape it is given.

How pipe bursting replaces the line

In pipe bursting, a cone-shaped head is pulled through the old pipe by a cable. It breaks the old pipe outward into the soil while drawing a new plastic pipe, usually HDPE, behind it. The result is a brand new pipe of the same or slightly larger size along the same path.

This suits old clay or cast iron lines that are too far gone for lining but still run in a fairly straight path. It also lets the plumber upsize a line when that makes sense.

Bursting does shake the ground. Any gas line, water service, or electric cable close to the sewer has to be located first, so utility locating, including the free 811 call, comes before any pit is dug. Ask how the plumber plans to protect neighboring utilities.

When digging is still the right call

These camera findings usually point away from a no dig fix.

  • ✓A section of pipe that has fully collapsed or been crushed flat
  • ✓A deep sag, called a belly, where water sits and solids settle
  • ✓A joint that has pulled apart or shifted so far that a liner cannot pass
  • ✓Pipe that slopes the wrong way or was laid with too little fall
  • ✓A broken connection at the main or a failed fitting that needs direct access
  • ✓Orangeburg pipe that has gone oval, since it may not hold a liner or survive bursting

What disruption looks like on a small lot

Many Bergen County lots are small, with a driveway on one side and a mature tree or two between the house and the street. A full trench can mean cutting the driveway, moving shrubs, and leaving a muddy strip for weeks. A no dig job usually needs one pit near the house and one near the property line or curb.

Plan for noise from a pump or compressor, a truck parked near the cleanout, and a day or two without usable plumbing in some cases. You may be asked to avoid running water while a liner cures. Ask in advance how long that window lasts.

Whatever the method, a licensed plumber should explain how the work connects at the street. If the problem sits in the public section of the line, the municipality or sewer authority may be involved. Ask the Englewood Building & Code Enforcement office, because permit rules vary by job.

What a typical no dig repair day looks like

  1. 01

    Locate and clean

    The plumber marks the line from above, clears roots and debris, and records a fresh camera run.

  2. 02

    Open small access points

    One or two pits go in at the cleanout and at the far end of the damaged section, with soil kept on a tarp.

  3. 03

    Install the liner or new pipe

    The liner is cured, or the new pipe is pulled in behind the bursting head.

  4. 04

    Reopen connections and re-scan

    Side lines are cut open, then a final camera pass confirms a clear, smooth pipe.

  5. 05

    Backfill and restore

    Pits are filled and compacted, and the plumber explains what surface repair, if any, you still need to arrange.

Check a plumber before you hire

New Jersey law says plumbing work must be done by a licensed master plumber. Ask for the licence number, then confirm it with the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers, part of the New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs.

Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Englewood Building & Code Enforcement office before work begins.

Questions homeowners ask

A properly cured liner or a new HDPE pipe is meant to last for decades, but the length of life depends on the condition of the rest of the line. A liner does not fix problems outside the section it covers. Ask your plumber what the camera showed on the stretches that were not repaired.
