What to Do When Pipes Freeze: Thaw, Shut Off, and Prevent in New Jersey

If a pipe freezes, open the nearest faucet, then warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer or warm towels, working from the faucet toward the frozen spot.

Call (551) 249-3045
By the Englewood Pro Plumbing teamPublished October 30, 2026Updated October 30, 20267 min read

If a pipe freezes, open the nearest faucet, then warm the pipe slowly with a hair dryer or warm towels, working from the faucet toward the frozen spot. Never use an open flame, a torch, or a propane heater. If you see a crack, a bulge, or water leaking, shut off the main water valve and call a licensed plumber.

In Englewood, NJ, the pipes most at risk run through exterior walls, unheated garages, crawl spaces, and basements near the foundation. Acting early matters, since ice can split a pipe before you notice.

Thaw a frozen pipe safely

  1. 01

    Find the frozen section

    No water or a trickle from a faucet is the first sign. Feel pipes on exterior walls and in cold spaces for frost or a very cold section.

  2. 02

    Open the faucet

    Open the tap that the frozen pipe feeds, hot and cold. Water has to be able to flow out as the ice melts, and it relieves pressure.

  3. 03

    Apply gentle heat

    Use a hair dryer, a heating pad, or towels soaked in hot water. Start near the faucet and move toward the blockage.

  4. 04

    Keep watching for leaks

    As ice melts, a split can start to spray or drip. Be ready to close the main shutoff.

  5. 05

    Keep the water running a bit

    Once water flows, leave the tap open slightly for a while, then check other fixtures for ice.

What never to use

Do not use a torch, a lighter, a kerosene or propane heater, or any open flame on a pipe. Heat that is too fast and too local can boil the water trapped in the ice, crack the pipe, or ignite the framing and insulation behind it.

Do not use a hot electric heat gun on plastic pipe, and do not stand in water while using electric tools. If the area is wet, keep electrical devices out of it.

Where pipes freeze and what to do

Freezing follows cold air, not just cold weather. This table covers the usual trouble spots.

Shut off the water before a thaw turns into a flood

A frozen pipe is a plug of ice, and the ice itself often holds the crack closed. When the ice melts, water pours through the split. That is why many burst pipes are found after the thaw, not during the freeze.

Know where your main shutoff is before you need it. It is usually near where the water line enters the house, often in the basement, a utility room, or near the water meter. Turn it clockwise to close it, and test it once a year so it is not stuck.

If you see water or a visible crack, close the main valve, open the lowest faucet to drain the lines, and call for burst pipe repair. If water is near outlets, the panel, or appliances, stay out and shut off the power at the breaker only if you can reach it dry.

How a pipe bursts

Water expands when it freezes. The surprise is where the pipe breaks. Ice forms in one place, and as it grows it traps water between the ice and a closed faucet. That trapped water has nowhere to go, so pressure builds and the pipe splits, often some distance from the ice itself.

Copper, steel, and plastic pipe all can split, though plastic such as PEX tolerates freezing better than rigid copper. That does not mean it is safe. Fittings can still pull apart.

Small splits can run for hours before anyone notices. Check ceilings, walls, and the floor below any pipe that froze, and watch the water meter. If it moves while everything is off, water is leaking somewhere.

Prevent frozen pipes before the next cold snap

Most of these cost little and take an afternoon.

  • ✓Insulate exposed pipes in the basement, garage, and crawl space with foam sleeves
  • ✓Disconnect garden hoses and close the interior valve to outdoor spigots
  • ✓Seal air leaks around pipes where they pass through walls and rim joists
  • ✓Open cabinet doors under sinks on exterior walls during hard freezes
  • ✓Let a faucet drip slowly on the coldest nights
  • ✓Keep the thermostat set no lower than about 55 degrees Fahrenheit when away
  • ✓Know where your main shutoff valve is and make sure it turns

When the problem is outside the house

If no water comes out of any tap, the freeze may be in the service line between the street and the house. That is the pipe that brings water in, and it is buried below ground. If it is frozen or damaged, it needs a plumber's attention, since thawing it can involve special equipment.

Ask your water supplier who owns which part of the service line, since responsibility often divides at the curb or the meter. A leak on your side is yours to repair. A licensed plumber can look at water service line repair options if the line is damaged.

As a general industry ballpark, a burst pipe repair inside the house often runs roughly $250 to $1,000 for the plumbing, and water damage cleanup is a separate cost. Service line work can run into several thousand dollars, depending on length and digging. These are wide, rounded ballparks and not a quote.

Insurance and what to document

Homeowner's policies often cover sudden water damage from a burst pipe, while slow leaks and wear usually are not. Ask your carrier what applies to your policy.

Neighbors in Tenafly and Leonia deal with the same claim steps. Take photos or video of the damage before you clean up, keep receipts, and save the broken pipe section if you can. A plumber's written description of what failed helps with a claim.

Check a plumber before you hire

New Jersey law says plumbing work must be done by a licensed master plumber. Ask for the licence number, then confirm it with the State Board of Examiners of Master Plumbers, part of the New Jersey Division of Consumer Affairs.

Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Englewood Building & Code Enforcement office before work begins.
