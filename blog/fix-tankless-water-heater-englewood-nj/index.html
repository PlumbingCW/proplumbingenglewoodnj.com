How to Fix a Tankless Hot Water Heater: What to Check Before You Call

Most tankless water heater problems come from a short list: scale on the heat exchanger, clogged inlet screens, a low flow rate, a gas or power interruption, or a venting fault that triggers an error code.

Most tankless water heater problems come from a short list: scale on the heat exchanger, clogged inlet screens, a low flow rate, a gas or power interruption, or a venting fault that triggers an error code. You can safely check the display code, the shutoff valves, the screens, and whether other fixtures run hot.

Anything that involves gas lines, venting, or the inside of the unit belongs to a licensed plumber, whether the job is a water heater repair or a full tankless water heater service. In Englewood, NJ, a unit that shows an error code, produces no hot water, or smells like gas should be shut off and looked at professionally.

Symptom, likely cause, and who handles it

Use this as a starting map. The right fix depends on the brand and model, so your manual comes first.

SymptomCommon causeWhat you can do
No hot water at all, display darkPower outage, tripped breaker, or loose cordCheck the outlet and the breaker. Call if power is fine
Display shows an error codeIgnition, flame, venting, sensor, or flow faultLook up the code in the manual. Reset once if the manual allows
Water runs lukewarm or fluctuatesScale, low flow, or too many fixtures running at onceClose other taps and retest. Ask about descaling
Hot water fades in a shower after a minuteFlow too low to fire the burner, or clogged filterClean the inlet screen if it is accessible
Burst of cold water between usesThe cold-water sandwich, a normal effectWait a few seconds. Ask about recirculation options
Smell of gas or exhaustGas leak or venting failureLeave the house and call the gas utility or 911

Gas smell is not a troubleshooting step. Leave first and call from outside.

Start with the display and the manual

Modern tankless units show a code when they detect a fault. The codes differ between brands, and the same number can mean different things on different models. Look up your code in the owner's manual or on the manufacturer's support page, not in a general list online.

Most manuals describe a reset step, usually turning the unit off and back on, or pressing a reset button. Try it once. If the same code returns, the unit is telling you something is actually wrong, and repeated resets will not fix a real fault.

Write the model number, the code, and what happened right before it. That information helps a technician bring the right parts, and it saves time on the phone.

The cold-water sandwich is not always a fault

If you shower, turn the water off briefly, and then turn it back on, you may get a short blast of cold. That is the cold-water sandwich. Water left sitting in the pipe cools off, and when you reopen the tap that cold slug reaches you before the heater has caught up.

Homeowners in Teaneck and Tenafly ask about this a lot. A small gap of cold water is normal with a tankless unit, and no repair will remove it. What helps is a recirculation pump or a small buffer tank, which keep hot water moving or stored near the fixtures. Ask a licensed plumber whether your unit supports that setup.

A long cold stretch, or hot water that never returns, is different. That points to a minimum flow problem, a clogged filter, or a fault, and it is worth a proper look.

Safe checks you can do yourself

  1. 01

    Confirm power and fuel are on

    Check the breaker or outlet for an electric unit. For gas, confirm the appliance gas valve is open and other gas appliances work.

  2. 02

    Test other fixtures

    If only one faucet is the problem, the fixture or its aerator may be clogged, not the heater.

  3. 03

    Check the inlet water filter

    Many units have a small screen where cold water enters. Close the isolation valves, release pressure per the manual, remove it, rinse it, and put it back.

  4. 04

    Look at the vent termination outside

    Make sure leaves, snow, or a nest are not blocking the exhaust or intake. Do not touch the flue pipe joints.

  5. 05

    Note the code and try a single reset

    Follow the manual. If the code comes back, stop and call.

Scale and flushing: the maintenance that matters most

Minerals in water build up as scale on the heat exchanger, the part that transfers heat from the burner to the water. Scale insulates the metal, so the unit works harder, flow drops, and error codes become more frequent. Over time it can shorten the life of the heater.

Manufacturers commonly recommend flushing the unit with a descaling solution on a regular schedule, often about once a year, though the right interval depends on your water and the model. The job uses a small pump, hoses, and a food-safe descaling liquid circulated through the unit through service valves.

If the unit has service valves, a plumber can flush it quickly. If it does not, adding them during the first visit makes every later flush easier. Skipping the flush is the single most common reason a tankless heater fails earlier than expected.

Leave these to a licensed plumber

These problems involve gas, combustion, or the sealed inside of the unit.

  • ✓Any smell of gas, or a suspected gas leak
  • ✓Soot, scorch marks, or a sharp exhaust smell near the unit
  • ✓Repeated ignition or flame-failure codes after a reset
  • ✓Water leaking from inside the cabinet
  • ✓Venting that looks loose, disconnected, or corroded
  • ✓A carbon monoxide alarm sounding

Carbon monoxide alarm: leave first

If a carbon monoxide alarm goes off, get everyone out of the house, including pets, and call 911 from outside. Do not stop to investigate the heater.

Fuel-burning appliances with poor venting are a classic source. Do not use the water heater again until a licensed professional has found the cause.

Water supply and scale: what Bergen County homeowners should know

Much of northern Bergen County drinks water from Veolia's Oradell Reservoir system, so check the supplier named on your water bill, and ask that supplier for its annual water quality report. That report is the right place to learn about your water, because scale depends on what is in it.

Veolia's system treats reservoir water at the Haworth Water Treatment Plant, and more than 885,000 residents and businesses in Bergen and Hudson counties depend on the system. Water quality varies by supplier, so do not assume your tap matches a neighbor's. If your supplier's report shows mineral content that concerns you, ask the plumber about a descaling schedule or a sediment filter ahead of the heater.

Check a plumber before you hire

Permit rules vary by job, so ask the Englewood Building & Code Enforcement office before work begins.
