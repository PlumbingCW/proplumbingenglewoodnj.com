A dripping spout wastes water slowly, but a valve that will not shut off can flood a room in minutes. Faucet repair in Englewood, NJ covers fixing or replacing the faucet, its cartridge, the supply valves and the lines under the sink so the fixture runs and stops the way it should. Emergency calls are taken 24/7.

If a faucet or its shutoff valve is spraying, close the main water valve, usually near the meter or where the line enters the house. Then open a low faucet to drain the pipes and put a bucket under the leak. Keep the main closed until a plumber looks at it, and leave the fixture alone.

What happens on a faucet repair visit

  1. 01

    Find the leak source

    The plumber checks whether water comes from the spout, handle, base, supply line or the valve under the sink.

  2. 02

    Shut off and open it up

    Water is closed at the fixture stop or the main, then the handle and cartridge are removed.

  3. 03

    Match the parts

    Cartridges, seats and O-rings must fit the brand and age of the faucet, so the old parts are checked first.

  4. 04

    Replace or repair

    Worn pieces are swapped. If parts are no longer made or the body is corroded, replacement is offered.

  5. 05

    Test under pressure

    The faucet runs hot and cold, the stops are checked, and connections below are inspected for drips.

Cartridges, seats and why faucets drip

Most modern faucets use a cartridge, a sealed insert that controls flow and temperature. When it wears, you get drips from the spout or a handle that feels loose or gritty. Older compression faucets use rubber washers pressed onto a brass seat, and the seat itself can pit over time.

A leak at the base of the handle usually points to an O-ring or packing. A leak from the spout when the faucet is off points to the cartridge or seat. Matching the right part matters, because many cartridges look alike but have different splines and lengths.

Supply valves that will not shut off

Under every sink sit two small stops, one hot and one cold. They are meant to isolate the fixture, but old ones often stay open for years and then seize. Turning a stuck stop hard can crack its stem or start a drip that does not stop.

A broken or leaking supply valve usually means closing the main valve, draining the line, cutting out the old stop, and fitting a new quarter-turn valve with braided stainless lines. If the pipe behind the wall is also aged, a plumber may suggest checking for hidden leaks before closing the job.

Shower valves, tub faucets and slow drains

A showerhead that leaks at the threads often needs fresh thread tape or a new washer. A shower that drips from the head after the handle is off, or one that swings between hot and cold, points to the cartridge or mixing valve inside the wall. Plumbers usually reach it through the handle trim, but older valves may need an access panel on the other side of the wall.

Tub faucets add a diverter, the pull-up knob or lever that sends water to the shower. When water pours from the tub spout while showering, the diverter has worn out. It is a modest repair unless the spout is corroded onto the pipe.

Hair and soap build into a plug just below the strainer, and most slow drains start there. Lift out the strainer and remove what you can reach by hand with gloves. Chemical drain cleaners are a poor choice because they can sit in the tub, harm finishes and give you a hazard if a plumber must open the trap later.

If water still backs up, the blockage is deeper in the branch line, and a plumber can clear it with a cable or look at it with a camera. See drain cleaning in Englewood for the process.

When a faucet or valve leak is an emergency

Treat it as urgent when a valve cannot be shut, water sprays from a pipe or fitting, or a leak is dripping through a ceiling below. A steady drip into a basin can wait for a scheduled visit, but a running line cannot.

Never stand in water to reach the panel or touch outlets near the flood. If the room is wet and the power is on, stay out and call.

What affects the price of faucet repair

A cartridge swap and a full valve replacement are very different jobs, so cost follows the work found behind the fixture.

Repair or replace

Fixing a cartridge takes less time than removing and installing a new faucet and supply lines.

Valve type and location
